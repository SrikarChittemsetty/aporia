Whoever supposes the words good and wicked were let down from heaven, graven and unalterable, has not read the chronicles. Among the old warrior peoples, good meant the noble, the strong-handed, the man who takes; and their word for the wicked was the same word they used for the common man. It was the trodden-down, the priestly and the rancorous, who struck a new coinage, stamping the strong man's virtue as sin and their own patience as merit. That money, being long in circulation, is now taken for gold dug from the mine. Valuations have their pedigrees as families do, and the proudest of them are of recent and doubtful descent.
