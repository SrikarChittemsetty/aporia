Men think themselves at liberty because they are conscious of their appetites and wholly ignorant of the causes by which those appetites are determined. A stone set flying by another body would, if it had a mind, believe as it went that it continued its flight of its own choosing. Thus the drunkard supposes he utters by free decree what afterwards, being sober, he wishes unsaid; the infant believes it freely desires the breast, and the coward believes he freely runs. The prejudice is common enough, for experience shows a man his wishes plainly while the chain of causes lies out of sight; and what lies out of sight is readily supposed not to be there.
