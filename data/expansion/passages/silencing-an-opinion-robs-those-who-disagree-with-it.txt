We are apt to suppose the injury falls chiefly upon the man whose mouth is stopped. It falls far heavier elsewhere. Should the suppressed opinion be true, the age is deprived of the opportunity of exchanging error for truth; and should it be false, the age loses what is well nigh as great a benefit, the clearer and livelier impression of truth produced by its collision with error. A creed that is never contested sinks into a dead formula, kept as a family heirloom is kept, without any knowledge of its worth. The arithmetician satisfies himself by working the sum backwards; but he whose opinion no man assails has no such proof, and holds his conviction upon trust.
