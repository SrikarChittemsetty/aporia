Ask, before the deed, whether the principle upon which you propose to proceed could stand as a law for every rational being. A man in straits resolves to borrow money on a promise he knows he cannot keep. Let him imagine his rule made general: promises would be given as lightly as they are broken, and the whole practice of promising must perish, together with the very advantage he sought by it. His maxim, thus enlarged, devours itself. What cannot without contradiction be thought as a universal law can never be the ground of a good will, however convenient it appear in the single case.
