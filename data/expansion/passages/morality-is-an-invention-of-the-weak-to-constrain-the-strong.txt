Nature herself declares, I think, that it is fitting for the better man to have more than the worse, and the abler than the feebler. Look to the beasts, and to whole cities and races of men: the stronger takes, and nature's own decree stands written in the deed. But among us the many, being feeble, mould the young as men break horses, chanting from the cradle that grasping is shameful and that having an equal share is fine and righteous. So they bind the lion's whelp with charms and incantations before his strength comes upon him. It is a contrivance of the herd, framed to hold in awe the one man who would otherwise stand above them.
