There is one plain rule by which the dealings of society with the individual may be governed. His own good, whether bodily or moral, is not a sufficient warrant. He may be remonstrated with, reasoned with, persuaded, entreated; he may not be compelled, nor visited with any evil for refusing. The man who ruins his health with drink, or squanders his estate upon follies, becomes answerable to us only when his conduct reaches beyond the circle of himself: when the drunkard strikes his wife, or the spendthrift leaves his creditors unpaid, we deal with him not as a fool but as a wrongdoer. Over that part of his conduct which touches himself alone, his independence is of right absolute.
