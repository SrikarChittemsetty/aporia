A miracle is a violation of those settled courses which the uniform experience of every age has established; and uniform experience is itself a proof, entire and complete. Set now against this proof the bare word of men. Men are credulous; they love the wonderful; they repeat with pleasure whatever astonishes their neighbours, and the heat of religion inflames the appetite further. Falsehood in a witness is a common thing; the failure of nature is not. When one tells me that the dead has risen, I weigh which is the likelier: that he deceives or is deceived, or that the whole frame of things has broken. The lighter wonder must always be preferred.
