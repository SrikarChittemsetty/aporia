They tell us that a man's willing is fixed by causes reaching back beyond his cradle, and yet that he is justly hanged for what he wills. But what praise belongs to the river for running to the sea, or what blame to the mill-wheel for turning as the water drives it? We do not arraign the clock for striking, though its stroke is as certain as any theft. If the felon's hand was moved by a chain whose first link was forged before ever he drew breath, then to punish him is not justice but husbandry: we beat him as we beat a mule, for the effect it works, not because he could have done otherwise.
