Consider in what estate men stand before ever a commonwealth is erected among them. Nature has not set one over another as she has set the shepherd over the flock: they are creatures of the same rank and the same faculties, born to the same advantages, sharing one common light of reason. There is no mark upon any brow, no crown grown out of any skull, no lion begotten among sheep. Each therefore orders his own actions and disposes of his own goods as he thinks fit, asking leave of no man, so far as the law written in reason permits him. Whatever authority came afterwards must have come by agreement, for nature herself conferred none.
