A man deliberating fancies he stands at a fork with two roads equally open to him; but appetite and aversion succeed one another in him as waves in a narrow channel, and whichever happens to be uppermost when the hour of action strikes, that he calls his choosing. The last appetite is the will, and it is no more elected than the last drop that breaks the vessel. Ask him why he preferred as he did, and he must name some good that appeared to him; ask why it so appeared, and he must name his temper, his breeding, his present hunger. His fancied sovereignty is nothing but ignorance of the reins.
