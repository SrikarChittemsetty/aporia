Men are wont to suppose that where the rulers are the people themselves, there remains no oppression to be guarded against. Yet society, when it sets itself up as tyrant, commands engines more searching than any magistrate's. A prince may seize a man's goods and imprison his body; the multitude enters his hearth, chooses his companions, and lays its hand upon his very opinions, leaving no corner in which a dissentient may draw breath. Its sentences are executed by no headsman, but by the averted face, the withheld employment, the general contempt. Fetters of that kind sit closer than iron, for they are worn within, and the sufferer comes at length to forget that he is bound.
