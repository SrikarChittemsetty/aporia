Let a man be persuaded in earnest that when he struck the blow no other course stood open to him, that his arm moved as the hammer moves when the smith drives it, and observe what becomes of his remorse. He may lament the deed as he laments a fever or a shipwreck; he cannot upbraid himself for it, since no man upbraids the hammer. Remorse is not bare sorrow. It carries within its very substance the thought, the road lay open both ways and I took the fouler; and every reproach we address to ourselves is framed upon that supposition. Take the supposition away and there remains nothing but grief at a misfortune, such as any beast might feel.
