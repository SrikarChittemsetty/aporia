Men speak as though there dwelt within the breast a separate faculty, an oracle delivering decrees of its own upon what is just. Examine rather what its decrees forbid: the taking of a life, the breaking of faith, the withholding from the labourer of his hire, the punishing of the innocent. These are no scattered prohibitions gathered at random. They are precisely the rules which guard the securities without which existence is a torment, for a man who cannot reckon upon tomorrow's bread possesses nothing whatever. That these maxims stand nearest to the foundations of our welfare, and draw no authority from some other source, is what lends them their peculiar sternness and their hold upon every conscience.
