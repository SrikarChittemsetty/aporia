Walk abroad and observe with what exactness the parts of the world are fitted to their offices. The eye is furnished with a lens, a chamber of clear humour, and a curtain that widens in the dusk and draws close in the noon — contrivances which the optician imitates and cannot better. Observe the wing set against the thinness of air, the fin against the weight of water, the teeth of the beast suited to the herb or the flesh he must live upon. Wherever means are adjusted to an end with such nicety, we never conclude that chance laid them so; we conclude a hand, a purpose, and an understanding that went before the work.
