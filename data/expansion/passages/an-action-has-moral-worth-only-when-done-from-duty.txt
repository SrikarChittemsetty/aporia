Consider the tradesman who deals fairly with the ignorant child sent to his shop, charging no more than he charges any other. Thus far his conduct is right; but he does it because his custom would suffer were the cheat once discovered. Here is prudence wearing the dress of honesty. Consider instead the man whom sorrow has made weary of living, who wishes for death and yet preserves his life, not from any liking for it, nor from fear, but because he ought. In the first the deed merely agrees with the rule; in the second alone does the deed carry its worth within itself.
