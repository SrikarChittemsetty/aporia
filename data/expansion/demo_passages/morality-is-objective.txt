Suppose a man in want resolves to borrow money upon a promise he knows he cannot keep. Let him ask, not what suits his present need, but what must follow were this made the standing rule of all men. Promising itself would perish, as coin perishes when every mint is free to debase it; none would lend, and the very instrument he meant to use would be gone out of the world. The maxim destroys itself upon being made universal, and reason discovers this without consulting his fortune or his liking. Thus the law that binds him is not fashioned by his wishes but found, as the geometer finds his propositions.
