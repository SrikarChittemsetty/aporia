Consider a man who must cross a torrent upon a plank in the dark. He has no proof the timber will bear him; he can measure nothing, weigh nothing. Shall he sit upon the bank till morning, with the flood rising at his back? To refrain from stepping is itself a choice, and the worse one. So it stands wherever the wager cannot be declined: reason falls silent, yet we must stake something, and he who declines to stake has staked already. Where the loss is little and the winning without measure, the prudent man ventures. He is no fool for wanting a demonstration; he were a fool to sit waiting for it.
