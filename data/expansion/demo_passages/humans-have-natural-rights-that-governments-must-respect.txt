Before ever a magistrate was set up, men were not therefore beasts without title. The husbandman who breaks the common ground, mingling his sweat and his labour with what lay wild, has joined somewhat of himself unto it, and no neighbour may justly carry it off, though no seal nor parchment attest the matter. Whence it appears that what a man holds in his own person, and in the work of his hands, is elder than the commonwealth, and was never the gift of it. Magistrates are stewards, not proprietors; and when the steward turns robber upon the house he was trusted to keep, the trust is forfeit and falls back to those who gave it.
