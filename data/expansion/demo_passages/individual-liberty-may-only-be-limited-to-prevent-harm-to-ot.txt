A man may ruin his health with drink, squander his patrimony, or preach a creed his neighbours hold detestable, and the magistrate has no title to lay a finger upon him for it. That his conduct will leave him poorer, or coarser, or a fool, is reason for remonstrance, for entreaty, for the plain speech of friends; it is never reason for the constable. Over that which concerns himself alone, his own body and his own mind, his sovereignty is entire. But let his cart be driven across another's field, or his brawl break a neighbour's window, and he is answerable; for there he has passed the fence of his own ground.
