Consider what sort of thing it is that dissolves. Whatever is compounded of parts is the thing that may come apart, as a lyre falls into wood and gut and string; but that which is single and uncompounded has no seams at which to be undone. Now the body is various, and alters daily, and is akin to what we see and handle. But that in us which reasons upon the equal itself and the beautiful itself lays hold of what is always the same, and is most like the divine and the indissoluble. And what is kindred to the deathless will not be scattered by the wind when the breath departs, but goes away whole.
