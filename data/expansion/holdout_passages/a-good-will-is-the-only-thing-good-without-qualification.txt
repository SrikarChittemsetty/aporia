Search the world through, and beyond it too, and you shall find nothing which may be called good absolutely and in every case, unless it be a rightly disposed will. Understanding, wit, judgment, and the other talents of the mind, courage also and constancy of purpose, are in many respects desirable; yet they become exceedingly hurtful when the volition which employs these gifts of nature, and whose peculiar constitution we name character, is itself crooked. Observe that the composure of a scoundrel makes him not merely more to be feared than a hot-blooded one, but more loathsome in our eyes; whence it appears that such qualities borrow their whole worth from the resolve that wields them.
