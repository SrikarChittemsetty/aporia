Pity is the contrary of those bracing affections which raise the tension of life; it works as a narcotic. A man loses power when he pities; suffering itself becomes contagious, and the multiplication of misery is dignified with a holy name. And that meekness which bows its head and calls itself lowly — examine it closely, and you will smell beneath it the instinct of the herd, the cunning of the weak, who, being unable to lift the hammer, declare the anvil sacred. Such moralities are no flowering of a healthy tree; they are the pale fungus that prospers only upon rotting wood, and they betray what has already begun to die.
