In that first condition every man is executioner of the law of reason, and thence arise inconveniences not to be borne. For self-love makes a man tender of his own cause and his friends', while anger and revenge carry him too far against another's; and the very party to the quarrel sits also upon the bench. Who would be a fair umpire in a race that he himself is running, or trust a merchant to weigh out his own goods with his own hand upon the scale? It is unreasonable that any should determine a controversy wherein his interest lies; and for the remedy of this partiality men quit that condition and set up a common judge.
