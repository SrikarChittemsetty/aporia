When those set to watch over a city come to possess lands and houses and coin, they will be stewards and farmers instead of guardians, and hostile masters of their fellows rather than allies. Riches and virtue lie in the two scales of a balance, and as the one rises the other must sink. For the man busy about gain grows careless of the good; he honours the treasury above the temple, praises the wealthy, and advances them to office, until the city is governed by men skilled in nothing but getting. Therefore let those who keep the whole be forbidden so much as to handle gold, lest in the end they steal the city itself.
