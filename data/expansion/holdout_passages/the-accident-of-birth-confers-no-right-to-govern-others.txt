Nature herself disallows the doctrine of hereditary right, else she would not so often mock it by setting a dullard where a lion was wanted. The pedigree of kings has no heavenly original: the first of them was no better than the head bandit of some restless troop, whose brutality won him the name of chief, and whose successors have gilded the theft with a chronicle. To say that a man is born the governor of others is as much as to say he is born a physician or a shipwright, and that the passengers must yield the helm to whoever came of the right womb, though he knows neither chart nor compass. Wisdom is not entailed upon posterity.
