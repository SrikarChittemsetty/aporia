Let a man of the strongest understanding be brought all at once into the world. He sees one billiard ball roll against another, and however narrowly he watches, he perceives only the one motion and then the other. He finds in the case no tie, no cement, no invisible cord binding the second to the first. Nor does the hundredth trial discover to him anything the first did not; the events remain loose and separate, conjoined but never bound. What alters is not the objects but the mind, which, being carried by custom from the one to the other, feels its own easy passage, and mistakes that feeling for a power lodged in the bodies themselves.
