That so few now dare to be singular is the chief peril of our age. Just because the despotism of custom has made mere strangeness a reproach, it is the more needful that men should be strange; for the quantity of oddity in a people has commonly been proportioned to its genius and its courage. Consider that great and ingenious nation of the East, which made all its citizens alike by one set of maxims, and which has therefore stood still a thousand years, and must be improved, if at all, by foreigners. A man is not a machine built after a pattern, but a tree, which requires to grow and spread itself on every side.
