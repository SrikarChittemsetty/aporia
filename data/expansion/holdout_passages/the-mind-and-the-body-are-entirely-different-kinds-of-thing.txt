I find within myself two notions plainly diverse. On the one hand I have a clear and distinct conception of myself as a thing which thinks and is nowise extended; on the other, of body as a thing extended which does not think. Now whatever is extended may be divided: cut away a foot or a hand, and somewhat is taken from the whole. But when I turn upon the mind, I can distinguish in it no parts whatever; the whole of it seems to will, the whole to understand, the whole to perceive. From this alone it appears that these are of natures altogether unlike, and that the one might subsist without the other.
