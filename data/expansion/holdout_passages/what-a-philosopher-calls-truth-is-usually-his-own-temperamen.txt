Little by little it has come clear to me what every great system has thus far been: the private confession of its author, a memoir written without his knowledge. Behind all logic there stand valuations, the bodily demands of a certain manner of living. Ask of that severe fellow with his proofs what his digestion permitted him, what climate agreed with his nerves, what he feared in his youth and what he could never forgive. His arguments are the advocate's brief, drawn up after the verdict was already given. He does not find; he baptizes his own inclinations with a solemn name, and calls the christening knowledge.
